## 1. Background and Context

The Centre of Excellence in Structural Designing (CoE-SD) is an initiative of the Department of Civil Engineering, Guru Nanak Dev Engineering College (GNDEC), Ludhiana, supported by TATA. The Centre is envisioned as a dedicated experiential learning and knowledge-sharing facility that bridges the gap between theoretical understanding of structural engineering concepts and their practical application in the construction industry.

The fundamental idea behind establishing the Centre is to create an environment where students, faculty members, practicing engineers and other stakeholders can understand structural systems not merely through textbooks, drawings and conventional classroom presentations, but through physical demonstrations, interactive digital installations, visual explanations and practical learning experiences.

Structural engineering concepts such as reinforcement detailing, load transfer, structural behaviour, ductility, durability, construction quality and failure mechanisms are often difficult to visualise through conventional teaching methods. The Centre seeks to address this limitation by translating these concepts into tangible, visible and interactive learning experiences.

The initiative is intended to go beyond being a conventional laboratory or an exhibition space. It is envisaged as a continuously evolving educational resource that promotes scientific understanding, sound structural design practices, construction quality awareness and industry–academia interaction.

## 2. Vision and Core Concept

The vision of CoE-SD is to develop an experiential structural engineering learning environment where complex engineering concepts can be observed, visualised, explored, understood and applied.

The Centre is based on the following learning philosophy:

**Observe → Visualise → Interact → Understand → Apply**

This approach will guide the planning of its physical installations, digital content, demonstrations, educational activities and visitor experience.

Rather than presenting isolated structural engineering models or information panels, the Centre will establish a connected learning environment in which every installation communicates a specific engineering principle and contributes to a broader understanding of structural systems.

The proposed facility will combine:

- Physical structural demonstrations and life-size construction details.
- Interactive and three-dimensional digital visualisations.
- Educational displays explaining structural behaviour and construction practices.
- Demonstrations of reinforcement detailing and structural components.
- Visual documentation of construction defects, structural failures and their underlying causes.
- Learning resources and activities for students, professionals and other stakeholders.

The overall objective is to make structural engineering more accessible, visual, practical and engaging while promoting a deeper understanding of the importance of appropriate design and construction practices.

## 3. Proposed Physical Infrastructure and Demonstration Installations

The physical installations will form an important component of the Centre. The intention is to use actual construction details and appropriately designed demonstration models to communicate engineering principles that are otherwise difficult to understand through conventional teaching.

### 3.1 Life-Size Reinforced Concrete Structural Demonstration

A prominent feature of the Centre will be a physical reinforced concrete structural demonstration incorporating exposed reinforcement details.

The proposed installation will demonstrate the arrangement of reinforcement in structural members and provide an opportunity to understand how reinforcement is placed, anchored and connected within reinforced concrete construction.

The demonstration is intended to highlight:

- Reinforcement arrangement in beams and columns.
- Longitudinal and transverse reinforcement.
- Reinforcement spacing and concrete cover.
- Development length and anchorage.
- Lap splices and their appropriate locations.
- Beam–column junction detailing.
- Confinement reinforcement and transverse reinforcement arrangements.
- Ductile detailing principles and seismic-resistant construction practices.

The exposed reinforcement arrangement will help visitors appreciate the relationship between structural drawings and actual reinforcement placement at construction sites.

### 3.2 Structural Detailing and Construction Practice Demonstrations

The Centre will also incorporate physical demonstrations of important structural detailing and construction practices.

The emphasis will be on presenting correct construction practices alongside the engineering reasoning behind them. These demonstrations will help learners understand why specific detailing requirements are necessary and how inappropriate reinforcement placement or construction practices can affect structural performance.

Where appropriate, modular demonstration panels and physical detailing models will be used to communicate good construction practices and common deficiencies.

### 3.3 Structural Demonstration Frames and Models

Structural demonstration frames and models will be used to explain the behaviour of structural systems and the interaction between different structural components.

These installations will support the understanding of load paths, structural connectivity, member behaviour and the role of individual components in maintaining overall structural stability.

The models will complement the life-size demonstrations by providing a simplified representation of structural behaviour.

### 3.4 Construction Best-Practice Demonstration Walls

Modular demonstration walls are envisaged as another important component of the Centre.

These will provide opportunities to present construction-related practices, detailing arrangements and comparative demonstrations. The intention is to create a flexible display system that can accommodate different themes and be updated as new educational requirements emerge.

## 4. Digital and Interactive Learning Infrastructure

The Centre will integrate digital technology with physical demonstrations to create a more engaging and comprehensive learning environment.

The digital installations are not intended to function merely as information displays. Their primary purpose will be to enable visitors to understand the internal arrangement, behaviour and detailing of structural components through interactive visualisation.

### 4.1 Interactive Three-Dimensional Structural Column

One of the proposed key digital installations is an interactive three-dimensional structural column visualisation.

The concept involves a digital representation of a reinforced concrete column that allows learners to explore its internal reinforcement arrangement and construction details.

The visualisation is intended to demonstrate:

- Longitudinal reinforcement arrangement.
- Transverse reinforcement and ties.
- Reinforcement spacing.
- Concrete cover.
- Lap splice arrangements.
- Confinement reinforcement.
- Seismic and ductile detailing.
- Internal structural configuration through sectional and exploded views.

The installation is envisaged to provide an interactive experience in which different components can be explored and understood independently as well as in relation to the complete structural member.

### 4.2 Digital Displays and Horizontal Screens

Digital screens will be used to present supporting educational content and visual explanations.

The proposed content will include:

- Structural behaviour under different loading conditions.
- Structural failure mechanisms.
- Construction defects and their implications.
- Reinforcement detailing demonstrations.
- Structural engineering animations.
- Educational videos and explanatory visualisations.
- Good construction practices and quality-control considerations.

These displays will complement the physical installations and help explain the engineering principles underlying the demonstrations.

### 4.3 Integration of Physical and Digital Learning

An important aspect of the Centre is the integration of physical and digital learning resources.

For example, a physical reinforcement demonstration can be supported by an interactive digital visualisation showing the same structural component in three dimensions, explaining its internal arrangement and illustrating the purpose of individual reinforcement elements.

This combination will enable learners to move from observing an actual construction detail to understanding its internal configuration and engineering significance.

## 5. Learning Journey and Visitor Experience

The Centre is envisaged as a self-guided and interactive learning environment.

The installations will be organised to establish a meaningful learning journey rather than functioning as unrelated displays.

A visitor should be able to begin with a basic understanding of a structural component, observe its physical arrangement, explore its internal details through digital visualisation and subsequently understand the engineering principles governing its behaviour.

The proposed learning journey will follow the sequence:

1. **Observe:** Identify and examine physical structural components, reinforcement arrangements and construction details.
2. **Visualise:** Understand internal configurations, structural behaviour and detailing through digital representations and explanatory displays.
3. **Interact:** Explore three-dimensional models, digital content and demonstration installations.
4. **Understand:** Relate the observed details to structural design principles, construction requirements, durability and safety.
5. **Apply:** Develop the ability to recognise appropriate structural detailing and construction practices in practical engineering situations.

This approach is intended to make the Centre useful for both guided academic visits and independent exploration.

## 6. Target Stakeholders and Intended Beneficiaries

The Centre is intended to serve a broad range of stakeholders associated with structural engineering, construction and the built environment.

Its beneficiaries will include:

- Undergraduate civil engineering students.
- Postgraduate students and research scholars.
- Faculty members and academic institutions.
- Practicing structural engineers and design professionals.
- Architects and construction professionals.
- Contractors, site engineers and construction supervisors.
- Skilled construction workers and other field-level personnel.
- Industry representatives and technical organisations.

Different stakeholder groups will be able to engage with the Centre through academic visits, demonstrations, technical sessions, training programmes, workshops and collaborative activities.

Particular emphasis will be placed on communicating the importance of good structural detailing and construction quality to individuals directly involved in executing construction work.

## 7. Academic, Technical and Industry Engagement Activities

CoE-SD is intended to function as an active academic and technical resource rather than a static display facility.

The Centre will provide a platform for organising activities that connect structural engineering education with current industry practices and professional requirements.

The proposed activities include:

- Technical demonstrations and structured learning sessions.
- Expert lectures and interactive discussions.
- Workshops on structural design and detailing.
- Training programmes on construction quality and good practices.
- Student competitions and model-based learning activities.
- Industry–academia interaction programmes.
- Awareness sessions on structural safety and durability.
- Technical visits by students and professionals.
- Research-oriented demonstrations and collaborative activities.

The Centre will also provide opportunities to integrate practical demonstrations with relevant undergraduate and postgraduate courses, laboratory activities, student projects and research work.

## 8. Content Development and Periodic Upgradation

The Centre is envisaged as a continuously evolving learning facility.

Its physical installations, digital content and educational resources should remain relevant to developments in structural engineering, construction technology and industry practices.

The proposed approach includes periodic review and updating of educational material, with a suggested cycle of approximately six months for content review and an annual cycle for broader assessment and upgradation.

The content-development process will focus on:

- Reviewing the relevance and accuracy of existing educational material.
- Incorporating new structural engineering concepts and construction practices.
- Updating digital demonstrations and explanatory videos.
- Introducing additional physical demonstration modules where required.
- Incorporating feedback from students, faculty and industry professionals.
- Identifying opportunities for new technical collaborations.

This approach will help ensure that the Centre remains useful as an academic and professional learning resource over the long term.

## 9. Operationalisation and Management of the Centre

The discussion also recognised that the development of CoE-SD must extend beyond the physical installation of models, screens and demonstration structures.

The Centre needs to be operationalised as an organised learning facility with clearly defined academic and technical activities.

The operational framework will involve:

- Coordinated management of the physical facility and its installations.
- Development and maintenance of educational content.
- Planning of student visits and demonstration sessions.
- Organisation of technical programmes and industry interactions.
- Maintenance of physical and digital installations.
- Periodic review of learning activities and stakeholder feedback.
- Documentation of activities, developments and achievements.

The Centre will be coordinated by the Department of Civil Engineering through its designated Incharge, under the institutional and departmental framework of GNDEC.

## 10. Implementation Approach

The implementation of CoE-SD will involve translating the conceptual framework into a functional physical and digital learning environment.

With the designated hall ready for setting up the Centre, the immediate focus is on planning the arrangement of installations, coordinating the required infrastructure and progressing towards operationalisation.

The implementation will consider the following interconnected components:

1. **Physical infrastructure:** Arrangement and installation of structural demonstration frames, reinforcement models, modular displays and other physical learning resources.
2. **Digital infrastructure:** Installation and configuration of digital displays and interactive visualisation systems.
3. **Educational content:** Development of explanatory material, three-dimensional visualisations, animations, videos and supporting learning resources.
4. **Integration:** Coordinating the physical and digital installations to establish a connected learning experience.
5. **Operational readiness:** Preparing the Centre for academic visits, technical demonstrations, workshops and other educational activities.

The overall implementation will be guided by the objective of creating a coherent and functional facility rather than simply installing individual display items.

## 12. Expected Outcomes and Long-Term Impact

The Centre is expected to contribute to structural engineering education by providing an environment where theoretical concepts can be connected with practical construction details and structural behaviour.

The anticipated outcomes include:

- Improved understanding of reinforced concrete structural systems and reinforcement detailing.
- Greater awareness of appropriate construction practices and structural quality.
- Improved ability to visualise internal structural configurations.
- Better understanding of structural behaviour, failure mechanisms and durability.
- Enhanced experiential learning opportunities for undergraduate and postgraduate students.
- Greater interaction between academia and industry.
- Opportunities for technical training and professional development.
- Increased awareness of structural safety among construction-related stakeholders.
- Development of a resource facility for academic demonstrations, research and technical engagement.

In the longer term, CoE-SD is intended to emerge as a distinctive learning and demonstration facility within the Department of Civil Engineering, supporting the development of technically competent graduates and promoting a culture of quality-oriented structural engineering practice.

## 13. Concluding Perspective

The Centre of Excellence in Structural Designing represents an effort to transform the way structural engineering concepts are communicated and understood.

Its strength lies in combining physical demonstrations, interactive digital technology, explanatory content and experiential learning within a single integrated environment.

The discussion held on 18 November 2026 marked an important stage in taking this initiative forward, particularly with the designated space ready for setting up the Centre and the participation of representatives associated with TATA and Tata Steel.

The focus ahead is to translate the proposed concepts into meaningful physical and digital installations, establish the intended learning journey and develop an operational framework through which the Centre can continuously serve students, faculty members, professionals and the wider construction community.

The ultimate objective is to create a Centre that does not merely display structural engineering concepts but enables people to see, explore, understand and appreciate the engineering principles that contribute to safe, durable and well-designed structures.
